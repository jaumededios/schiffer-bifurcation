A counterexample to
Schiffer's conjecture

Let \(\Omega\subset\mathbb R^2\) be a smooth bounded domain, and suppose a nonconstant Laplace eigenfunction satisfies \(-\Delta u=\lambda u\) in \(\Omega\), while \(u\) is constant and \(\partial_\nu u=0\) on its boundary. Schiffer's conjecture says that these simultaneous boundary conditions should force \(\Omega\) to be a disk—a striking planar rigidity statement. The disk is indeed locally rigid; analogous problems on the sphere and flat cylinder, however, admit nontrivial bifurcations. We transfer that flexibility back to the plane: high rotational symmetry turns a planar quotient into a long cone, its rim approaches a half-cylinder, and a cone branch can reach an integer symmetry order and unfold into a noncircular planar domain.

01

The overdetermined eigenvalue problem

Which domains have the Schiffer property?

The boundary constant cannot vanish for a nontrivial solution, so we divide by it and obtain the normalized problem shown on the right. A domain carrying such a function is said to have the Schiffer property.

This is overdetermined: a second-order elliptic equation normally accepts either Dirichlet or Neumann data, not both. Nevertheless every disk has infinitely many radial solutions, one for each positive zero of J₁. Schiffer's conjecture asserted that these were the only bounded planar examples. The problem is therefore to construct a solution for a domain that is not a disk.

Schiffer(Ω) {
−Δu = λuin Ω,
u = 1on ∂Ω,
∂νu = 0on ∂Ω.

λ>0 and u is not constant.

Why a spectral coincidence appears

Linearizing the moving boundary produces a Dirichlet eigenfunction at the same eigenvalue.

Start from a symmetric Schiffer solution (Ω,u,λ). Move the boundary with normal velocity f, and write u̇=v, λ̇=μ. For a nonradial mode, the radial forcing μu disappears after angular projection.

{
(−Δ−λ)v = 0in Ω,
v = 0on ∂Ω,
∂νv = λfon ∂Ω.

The first two lines say that v is a Dirichlet eigenfunction with eigenvalue λ. The last line recovers the boundary displacement from it. Thus a nontrivial shape direction requires

λ ∈ SpecN,rad(Ω)∩SpecD,nonrad(Ω).
Derive the boundary conditionsexpand +

Pull the moving problem back to the original domain. Differentiating ut|∂Ωt=1 gives v+f∂νu=0, hence v=0 because the base solution is Neumann.

Differentiating the Neumann condition gives ∂νv+f∂ννu=0. Since u=1 and ∂νu=0 on the boundary, all tangential derivatives vanish there and the PDE yields ∂ννu=−λ. Therefore ∂νv=λf.

In the interior, differentiation gives (−Δ−λ)v=μu. On a rotationally symmetric base, u is radial; projecting onto any nonzero angular mode removes the right-hand side and gives the homogeneous Dirichlet eigenvalue equation above.

02

The Euclidean disk

Bourget's hypothesis blocks the bifurcation.

On the unit disk, a radial Schiffer eigenfunction has the form

u0(r)=J0(ρr), λ=ρ².

Its Neumann condition is

∂ru0(1)=−ρJ1(ρ)=0. Therefore J1(ρ)=0.

A possible deformation in angular mode ℓ would have to come from the Dirichlet eigenfunction

vℓ(r,θ)=Jℓ(ρr)cos(ℓθ), so Jℓ(ρ)=0.
Bourget's hypothesis

Distinct integer-order Bessel functions have no common positive zero.

Siegel proved Bourget's hypothesis: if m≠n are nonnegative integers, then Jm and Jn share no nonzero zero. Hence every candidate ℓ≥2 is impossible.

The exceptional mode ℓ=1 uses the same equation J₁(ρ)=0, but it is geometric noise rather than a new domain: ∂xu₀=−ρJ₁(ρr)cosθ is exactly the derivative obtained by translating the disk.

References: DLMF §10.21; Watson, A Treatise on the Theory of Bessel Functions, §15.28; Cao-Labora–de Dios Pont.

03

Change the ambient geometry

The spectral obstruction is not universal.

On the plane, changing the radius of a disk merely rescales every eigenvalue by the same factor, so no transverse spectral crossing can appear. The flat cylinder and the sphere break that scaling lock. In both settings genuine nonradial Schiffer branches are known.

flat cylinder

Nontrivial Schiffer domains exist on ℝ × S¹.

Fall, Minlend and Weth construct compact domains on the flat cylinder whose Neumann eigenfunctions are constant on the boundary. In our half-cylinder limit, 1<λ<4 leaves the first angular channel oscillatory while every k≥2 channel is evanescent.

Fall–Minlend–Weth, JEMS (2025) ↗
half-sphere

A spherical cap bifurcates in an eight-fold mode.

Cao-Labora and Fernández find a transverse meeting of a zonal Neumann branch with a nonzonal Dirichlet branch. Curvature separates the two spectral curves and produces smooth contractible Schiffer domains on the half-sphere.

Cao-Labora–Fernández (2025) ↗

Borrow the cylinder's flexible local problem, but package it inside an N-fold quotient that can eventually be unfolded back into the plane.

07

The computation along the bifurcating branch

First the radius moves. Then the phase follows.

Section 6 varied \(r_0\) at fixed \(\lambda\) to display the basis limit. The proof follows a different path: at a crossing \(J_1(\rho)=J_{R_*}(\rho)=0\), it keeps \(\rho\) fixed and solves for the cone radius \(R=R(s)\). We therefore make two computations in order: the nonlinear boundary equations determine \(R''(0)\), and the fixed-\(\rho\) Debye phase converts that radius change into a phase change.

01 / compute the radius drift

The second harmonic determines the sign.

Half-turn symmetry gives \(R(-s)=R(s)\), so \(R'(0)=0\). The quadratic product \(\cos^2\theta=\tfrac12(1+\cos2\theta)\) produces only modes \(0\) and \(2\). If \(d_*=(\partial_\nu j_{\nu,m})|_{\nu=R_*}\), the regular second harmonic and its boundary slope are

\[W_2(t)=-\frac{\rho^2}{4}\frac{J_{2R_*}(\rho t)}{J_{2R_*}(\rho)},\qquad S_2=W_2'(1)=-\frac{\rho^3}{4}\frac{J'_{2R_*}(\rho)}{J_{2R_*}(\rho)}.\]

At cubic order the first-harmonic solvability condition is \(V_1(1)=\rho^2/4-S_2/2=\mu Q(1)\), where \(Q(1)=-\rho d_*\) and \(\mu=R_*^2R''(0)/2\). Solving this scalar equation gives the exact curvature

\[R''(0)=-\frac{\sqrt{\lambda_*}}{4R_*d_*}\left(2+\rho\frac{J'_{2R_*}(\rho)}{J_{2R_*}(\rho)}\right).\]

For large \(R_*\), the order derivative and the mode-2 logarithmic derivative have Debye limits. Hence

\[R''(0)=-\Gamma(\lambda_*)+O(R_*^{-1}),\qquad \Gamma(\lambda)=\frac{\sqrt{(\lambda-1)(4-\lambda)}}{4\arccos(\lambda^{-1/2})}>0.\]
02 / convert radius drift into phase drift

The phase moves by an explicit amount.

Along this branch \(\rho\) is fixed. The oscillatory Debye phase at the rim is

\[\xi(R;\rho)=\sqrt{\rho^2-R^2}-R\arccos\!\left(\frac R\rho\right)-\frac\pi4,\qquad \partial_R\xi=-\arccos\!\left(\frac R\rho\right).\]

Thus \(R\mapsto\xi\) is locally linear and invertible. But the signed branch parameter enters through an even function,

\[R(s)=R_*+\frac12R''(0)s^2+O(s^4).\]

The chain rule now gives the motion seen in the second graph:

\[\Delta\xi(s)=-\frac12\arccos\!\left(\frac{R_*}{\rho}\right)R''(0)s^2+O(s^4).\]

Because both factors on the right are negative, \(\Delta\xi(s)>0\): the cone radius decreases while the local cylinder phase advances. There is a linear relation between phase and \(R-R_*\), and a quadratic relation between either of them and \(s\).

the visible boundary wave · order \(s\)
\[r_{\partial}(\psi;s)=R(s)-s\cos\psi+O(s^2).\]

The two opposite points \(\psi=0,\pi\) move in opposite directions at speed one. This is the first-order wiggle visible in the cylinder and cone pictures.

the mean cone radius · order \(s^2\)
\[\frac1{2\pi}\int_0^{2\pi}r_{\partial}(\psi;s),d\psi=R_*+\frac12R''(0)s^2+O(s^4).\]

The cosine averages to zero. The parameter that must land on an integer therefore has no linear motion; its first nonzero change is the negative quadratic drift computed above.

The large-radius basis limit

Bessel modes become cylinder modes near the rim.

\(x=r-r_0\) \(r_0-5\le r\le r_0\) \(\lambda=2.4\) fixed

Let the cone have rim radius \(r_0\) and metric \(dr^2+(r^2/r_0^2)d\psi^2\). After separating the angular variable, the cone and half-cylinder have the bases below. The assertion is local: on the fixed coordinate \(x=r-r_0\), the coefficients of the cone radial equation converge to the constant coefficients of the cylinder equation as \(r_0\to\infty\).

01 / half-cylinder · \(x\le0\)

The separated cylinder basis

\[ f_k''+(\lambda-k^2)f_k=0,qquad u_k(x,\theta)=f_k(x)\{\cos(k\theta),\sin(k\theta)\}. \] \[ f_k(x)= \begin{cases} A_k\cos(\sqrt{\lambda-k^2}\,x)+B_k\sin(\sqrt{\lambda-k^2}\,x),&k^2<\lambda,\\ C_k e^{\sqrt{k^2-\lambda}\,x},&k^2>\lambda. \end{cases} \]

For \(1<\lambda<4\), the channels \(k=0,1\) oscillate. Boundedness as \(x\to-\infty\) leaves exactly one exponential for every \(k\ge2\).

02 / cone · regular at the tip

The separated cone basis

\[ f_k''+\frac1r f_k'+\left(\lambda-\frac{k^2r_0^2}{r^2}\right)f_k=0, \] \[ u_k(r,\psi)=J_{kr_0}(\sqrt\lambda,r) \{\cos(k\psi),\sin(k\psi)\}. \]

Regularity at \(r=0\) selects \(J_{kr_0}\) and rejects \(Y_{kr_0}\). There is no modified-Bessel replacement: \(I\) and \(K\) solve the equation with the opposite spectral sign.

The limit on a fixed collar

Put \(q=\sqrt\lambda\), \(\omega=\sqrt{\lambda-1}\), and \(\Phi(q)=\sqrt{q^2-1}-\arccos(q^{-1})\). Debye's oscillatory formula gives

\[J_{r_0}(\sqrt\lambda(r_0+x))=A_{r_0}(x)\cos\!\left(r_0\Phi(\sqrt\lambda)-\frac\pi4+\omega x\right)+O(r_0^{-1}).\]

Thus changing \(r_0\) translates the local wave by the explicit phase rate \(d\xi/dr_0=\Phi(\sqrt\lambda)\). For the higher channels, the nonoscillatory Debye formula gives

\[\frac{J_{kr_0}(\sqrt\lambda(r_0+x))}{J_{kr_0}(\sqrt\lambda r_0)}\longrightarrow e^{\sqrt{k^2-\lambda}\,x},\qquad k\ge2.\]

Numerical method

Exact inside, numerical on the wall.

The mathematical limiting problem is posed on the genuine half-infinite cylinder Ωλ,s={x<hλ,s(θ)}. Uniformly for λ in each compact K⋐(1,4), the same local bifurcation machinery used for compact flat-cylinder domains gives a one-ended branch with hλ,s=s cosθ+OK(s²). The finite window below is only how that infinite-domain solution is displayed in the browser.

We set Γs: x = hs(θ) = s cos(θ − φ) + h₂ cos 2(θ − φ) + h₃ cos 3(θ − φ). The mean translation is fixed to zero and h₁=s is the amplitude gauge. The field is a truncated sum of separated solutions of (Δ + λ)u = 0 in the physical cylinder coordinates.

The k = 0 and k = 1 radial factors are oscillatory. For k ≥ 2 they are e√(k²−λ)x cos(kθ) and e√(k²−λ)x sin(kθ), which decay as x → −∞. Consequently, the interior PDE residual is identically zero.

For the selected (λ,s), a damped Gauss–Newton solve with an analytic wall Jacobian updates the field coefficients, h₂, and h₃ simultaneously on at least 256 angular samples; a final column-scaled QR solve polishes the field at the recovered wall. Exponential modes are rescaled by constants to avoid overflow without changing their span. The reported defects are evaluated on a separate grid of at least 512 shifted samples in the normalized metric ||f||² = (2π)⁻¹∫|f(θ)|²dθ. With only two wall corrections and independent λ,s sliders, the result is a truncated free-boundary solve rather than an exact branch, so the defects need not vanish.

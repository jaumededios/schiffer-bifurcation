Separated solutions / radial freedom

Where the phase goes.

f″ + (λ − k²)f = 0 r²f″ + rf′ + (λr² − k²R²)f = 0 cylinder / cone

Fix one angular label k. On the flat half-cylinder, an oscillatory radial mode has two bounded basis functions, cosine and sine, so their ratio is a free phase. At the cone tip, regularity keeps only the ordinary Bessel branch JkR. The scalar freedom reappears geometrically: allow R to be real and changing the order moves the Bessel phase.

The critical comparison is k = 1. Move the shared dial from the integer cone R = 28 to the crossing R* = 28.026397…; alongside it, the cylinder combination rotates from cosine to sine.

01 / flat cylinder

Two oscillatory radial solutions

For k² < λ, the bounded radial space is span{cos(ωx), sin(ωx)}. Choosing their coefficient ratio is choosing a phase δ.

02 / cone tip

One tip-regular Bessel solution

Regularity discards YkR. There is no second tip-regular modified-Bessel companion for this positive-λ equation: I/K belong to the sign-flipped radial equation.

03 / relaxed order

The scalar freedom becomes R

Varying non-integer R changes the order kR, hence the radial Bessel phase. At R = R*, the critical mode reaches JR*(ρ*) = 0.

How to read the picture

The right edge is the unknown.

The vertical direction is the periodic angle θ. The horizontal direction runs inward along the cylinder. Everything to the right of the bright boundary lies outside the domain.

As λ approaches 4, the k = 2 correction decays more slowly: its natural length 1 / √(4 − λ) becomes large. This part is a genuine feature of the cylinder mode equation.

Numerical method

Exact inside, numerical on the wall.

We set Γs: x = hs(θ) = s cos(θ − φ). The field is a truncated sum of separated solutions of (Δ + λ)u = 0 in the physical cylinder coordinates.

The k = 0 and k = 1 radial factors are oscillatory. For k ≥ 2 they are e√(k²−λ)x cos(kθ) and e√(k²−λ)x sin(kθ), which decay as x → −∞. Consequently, the interior PDE residual is identically zero.

For the selected (λ,s), a column-scaled QR solve fits the boundary data using at least 256 angular samples. Exponential modes are rescaled by constants to avoid overflow; this does not change their span. The reported defects are evaluated on a separate grid of at least 512 shifted samples in the normalized metric ||f||² = (2π)⁻¹∫|f(θ)|²dθ. Independent sliders generally move off the true one-dimensional branch, so these defects need not vanish.

Cone continuation

The cone panel uses the metric dr² + (r²/R²)dψ². At fixed ρ = j1,16, the field and boundary are expanded in 13 even Fourier–Bessel modes. An offline nonlinear least-squares continuation enforces u = 1 and the exact metric normal derivative on 112 collocation angles; the displayed defects come from 512 shifted validation angles.

The browser interpolates those solved branch records and precomputed radial Bessel tables. At the final record R = 28, the quotient angle 2π/R tiles the plane exactly 28 times. The unfolded view draws the true seam angle and also includes a magnified inset because the initial mismatch is only about 0.339°.

Angular modes

The comparison panel evaluates the cylinder factors analytically and samples the cone factors from the precomputed normalized tables JkR(ρ*r/R). At the exact crossing knot, the critical trace is imposed as JR*(ρ*) = 0; interpolation is only between nearby stored radial samples and the two endpoint orders.
